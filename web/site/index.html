Tap Dodge Rush

The real rules of a shipped Google Play game, lifted into a module with no Android on its classpath and compiled to JavaScript by TeaVM.

drag, or ← → · space to start · music starts on your first tap

What differs from the Play build. The shipped game sells a continue through a rewarded ad. There are no ads here and free continues would make this score mean something other than the one on your phone, so the web build has none — the engine's canUseContinue() is simply never called.

What the Play Store has. That release predates the extraction and still carries the rules inline in the Android view — the module below is what the app builds against now, not what shipped in April. Same rules either way; this is the readable copy.

Why "one rulebook" is checkable. The rules live in one module with no Android and no TeaVM on its classpath. A test drives a scripted 600-frame game on the JVM, records the score, the player and every obstacle position at 11 checkpoints, and a Node script replays the identical run through this page's tapdodge.js. The two traces are diffed on every push: counts and scores exactly, positions to a hundredth of a pixel. A second job diffs the module against the copy in the app's own repo, so the two cannot drift apart unnoticed.

It was written because the builds had already drifted — the browser used to play a different game from the same seed — and it is checked in the state where reverting that fix turns it red.
